Around 1242 Ibn al-Nafis, a physician from Damascus who worked in Cairo, wrote a commentary on the anatomy in Avicenna's Canon of Medicine. In it he rejected Galen's pores. The wall between the ventricles is thick and solid, he wrote, and blood cannot pass through it.

He wrote that the blood of the [right ventricle](@right-ventricle) must go by the [pulmonary artery](@pulmonary-trunk) to the lungs, mix there with air, and return by the [pulmonary veins](@pulmonary-veins) to the [left side of the heart](@left-atrium). This is the short loop, described nearly 400 years before Harvey.

His commentary stayed almost unknown in Europe. There the same passage through the lungs was described again by Michael Servetus in 1553 and by Realdo Colombo in 1559. Harvey knew Colombo's account.
