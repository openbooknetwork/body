In 1661 Marcello Malpighi, professor of medicine at Bologna, looked at the [lung](@lungs) of a frog through a microscope. He saw that it was made of tiny air sacs at the ends of the [airways](@bronchi). In their walls he saw blood running through fine tubes from the [arteries](@pulmonary-arteries) into the [veins](@pulmonary-veins).

@image malpighi-abb878

These were the capillaries, the vessels Harvey could not see. Malpighi sent his findings as two letters to his friend Giovanni Borelli in Pisa, printed the same year as De pulmonibus. With the capillaries found, the route of the blood was complete. It runs from the heart through the arteries, capillaries and veins, and back to the heart.
