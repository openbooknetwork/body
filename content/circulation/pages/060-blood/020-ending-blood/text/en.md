A red cell has no nucleus and cannot repair itself. After about 120 days its membrane has stiffened. To leave the pulp of the [spleen](@spleen), a cell must squeeze through slits narrower than itself, and a stiff one gets stuck and is eaten by the macrophages waiting there.

@image spleen-gray-6ae2c7

The macrophage takes the haemoglobin apart. The iron is bound to a carrier protein and returned to the marrow, so the body loses only a milligram or two a day. The rest of the haem becomes bilirubin, a yellow pigment that travels in the [splenic](@splenic-vein) and [portal veins](@portal-vein) to the [liver](@liver).

The liver takes bilirubin out of the blood and passes it into the bile and so into the gut, where its products colour the stool brown. When the liver fails, or cells break down faster than it can clear them, bilirubin builds up and turns the skin yellow.
