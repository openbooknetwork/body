Blood is made inside bone. In a child every bone holds red marrow. In an adult it remains in the [skull](@skull), the [sternum](@sternum) and ribs, the [vertebrae](@vertebral-column), the [pelvis](@pelvic-girdle) and the upper ends of the [femurs](@femurs), while the shafts of the long bones fill with yellow fat.

@image bone-marrow-gray-0a9854

The marrow makes some two million red cells a second, about 200 billion a day, together with the white cells and the platelets. A red cell matures in about a week, loses its nucleus, and slips into the blood as a disc about 8 micrometres across, packed with haemoglobin.

@image blood-cells-sem-535b86

The kidneys set the pace. When the blood reaching them carries too little oxygen, they release the hormone erythropoietin, and the marrow speeds up. This is why people who live at high altitude have more red cells in their blood.
