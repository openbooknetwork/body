The heart muscle is too thick to feed on the blood inside its chambers. Two arteries, the [right](@right-coronary-artery) and the [left coronary](@left-coronary-artery), leave the aorta just above the aortic valve as the first branches of the systemic circulation. They run in the grooves on the heart's surface and send branches into the muscle.

The left coronary divides after one or two centimetres. Its [anterior interventricular branch](@anterior-interventricular-artery) runs down the front between the two ventricles, and its [circumflex branch](@circumflex-artery) curves round the left side to the back. Between them they feed most of the left ventricle.

The coronaries fill mostly between beats, when the muscle relaxes and stops squeezing them. At rest they carry about 4 percent of the heart's output. A heart attack happens when a clot blocks one of them. The muscle beyond the clot gets no oxygen, and after about 20 minutes without blood it begins to die.
