Four valves keep the blood moving one way. One valve lies between each atrium and its ventricle. The [tricuspid](@tricuspid-valve) on the right has three leaflets, the [mitral](@mitral-valve) on the left has two. Cords tie the leaflets to [small muscles](@papillary-muscles) in the ventricle wall, so they cannot turn back into the atrium when the ventricle squeezes.

@image valves-base-gray-0fb6f3

At the outlet of each ventricle sits another, the [pulmonary valve](@pulmonary-valve) on the right and the [aortic valve](@aortic-valve) on the left. Each has three cusps shaped like pockets, which fill and close as soon as blood starts to flow back.

@image aortic-valve-gray-ae40be

Through a stethoscope each beat sounds as two. The first sound is the tricuspid and mitral valves closing as the ventricles start to contract, the second the pulmonary and aortic valves closing as they relax. A murmur between them is blood forced through a valve that has narrowed or leaks.
