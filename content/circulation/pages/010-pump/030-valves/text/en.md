Four valves keep the blood going one way. Between each atrium and its ventricle sits one, the [tricuspid](@tricuspid-valve) on the right with three leaflets and the [mitral](@mitral-valve) on the left with two, each tethered by cords to the ventricle wall so that it cannot flip backwards under pressure. At the outlet of each ventricle sits another, the [pulmonary](@pulmonary-valve) on the right and the [aortic](@aortic-valve) on the left, three pockets that fill and close the moment the blood tries to come back.

Through a stethoscope a beat is two sounds. The first is the tricuspid and mitral valves closing as the ventricles begin to squeeze, the second the pulmonary and aortic valves closing as they relax. A murmur between them is blood forced through a valve that has narrowed or leaks.
