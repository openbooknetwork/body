The heart is two pumps side by side, each of two rooms. Blood from the body enters the [right atrium](@right-atrium), passes to the [right ventricle](@right-ventricle) below it and is sent to the lungs. Back from the lungs it enters the [left atrium](@left-atrium), passes to the [left ventricle](@left-ventricle) and is sent to the body.

The atria are thin-walled, since they only fill the ventricles below them. The left ventricle has the thickest wall of the four, 8 to 12 millimetres against 3 to 5 for the right, because it drives blood through the whole body while the right only drives it through the lungs. A wall of muscle, the septum, divides left from right, and after birth no blood crosses it.
