Each subclavian artery becomes the [axillary artery](@axillary-arteries) as it crosses the outer edge of the first rib, and the [brachial artery](@brachial-arteries) at the lower edge of the armpit. The brachial runs down the inner side of the upper arm close to the humerus. Pressing it against the bone stops bleeding further down the arm.

In front of the elbow it divides into the [radial](@radial-arteries) and [ulnar arteries](@ulnar-arteries). The radial runs down the thumb side of the forearm, and its pulse is the one felt at the wrist. The ulnar runs down the side of the little finger.

In the palm the two join in a [superficial](@superficial-palmar-arches) and a [deep palmar arch](@deep-palmar-arches), from which the fingers are fed. Because both arteries feed the arches, the hand keeps its blood supply when one of them is blocked.
