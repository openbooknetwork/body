The brain is about 2 percent of the body's weight and receives about 15 percent of its blood at rest. Four arteries bring it: the two [internal carotids](@internal-carotid-arteries) in front, and behind them the two [vertebral arteries](@vertebral-arteries), which join under the brainstem into the [basilar artery](@basilar-artery).

@image circle-willis-gray-91209c

At the base of the brain these join in a ring, the [arterial circle](@arterial-circle), described by Thomas Willis in 1664. From it the [anterior](@anterior-cerebral-arteries), [middle](@middle-cerebral-arteries) and [posterior cerebral arteries](@posterior-cerebral-arteries) spread over each hemisphere.

@image brain-territories-gray-56c6c5

The ring can carry blood from one side to the other if an artery is blocked, but in many people one of its links is thin or missing. Brain cells store almost no oxygen. When the blood flow to the brain stops, consciousness is lost within about 5 to 10 seconds.
