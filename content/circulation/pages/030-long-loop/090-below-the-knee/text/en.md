Behind the knee the [popliteal artery](@popliteal-arteries) lies deep in a hollow filled with fat, so its pulse is hard to feel. Just below the knee it divides into the [anterior](@anterior-tibial-arteries) and [posterior tibial arteries](@posterior-tibial-arteries).

@image popliteal-gray-3d3a2d

The anterior tibial passes forward between the two leg bones and runs down the front of the leg to become the [dorsalis pedis](@dorsalis-pedis-arteries) on the top of the foot. The posterior tibial runs down the calf, passes behind the inner ankle bone and divides under the foot into the [plantar arteries](@plantar-arteries) of the sole.

@image anterior-tibial-gray-f6820c

Doctors feel for a pulse on the top of the foot and behind the inner ankle to judge the blood supply of the foot. Standing adds the weight of the blood column: in a standing adult the arterial pressure in the feet is about 90 millimetres of mercury higher than at the heart.
