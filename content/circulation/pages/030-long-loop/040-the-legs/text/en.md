At the fourth lumbar vertebra the aorta ends in the two [common iliac arteries](@common-iliac-arteries). Each divides again: an [internal branch](@internal-iliac-arteries) into the pelvis, and the [external iliac](@external-iliac-arteries), which passes under the groin ligament and becomes the [femoral artery](@femoral-arteries), the pulse a hand finds at the top of the thigh. The femoral runs down the inside of the thigh, slips through the thigh muscles to the back of the knee as the [popliteal](@popliteal-arteries), and divides below it into the [anterior](@anterior-tibial-arteries) and [posterior tibial](@posterior-tibial-arteries) arteries, the second felt behind the inner ankle. The anterior crosses the front of the ankle as the [dorsalis pedis](@dorsalis-pedis-arteries), the pulse on the top of the foot, and the posterior passes under the heel to the [plantar arteries](@plantar-arteries) of the sole.

The arteries of the legs are the longest in the body. Blood that leaves the heart at a pressure of 120 millimetres of mercury reaches the toes a second later, still at about 110.
