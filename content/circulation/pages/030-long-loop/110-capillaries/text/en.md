Capillaries are the narrowest vessels, about 5 to 10 micrometres across, so red cells pass in single file and often bend to fit. Their wall is a single layer of thin flat cells. Oxygen, food and wastes pass through it between the blood and the fluid around the cells.

Each capillary is half a millimetre to a millimetre long, and the body has some 10 billion of them. Blood slows to about a third of a millimetre a second inside them, so it takes one to three seconds to cross.

In the hand the model's arteries end in the [palmar arches](@palmar-arches) and its veins begin in the [network on the back of the hand](@dorsal-venous-networks). Between them, too small for the model, lie the capillaries of the skin, muscles and bones of the hand.
