At the fourth lumbar vertebra the aorta ends in the two [common iliac arteries](@common-iliac-arteries). Each divides in front of the joint between the sacrum and the hip bone. The [internal iliac](@internal-iliac-arteries) feeds the pelvis and the buttock, and the [external iliac](@external-iliac-arteries) runs on towards the leg.

Under the inguinal ligament the external iliac becomes the [femoral artery](@femoral-arteries). Its pulse can be felt in the groin, halfway between the front point of the hip bone and the pubic symphysis. A few centimetres lower it gives off the [deep femoral artery](@deep-femoral-arteries), which feeds most of the thigh muscles.

The femoral artery runs down the front and inner side of the thigh. Above the knee it passes through a gap in the adductor muscles to the back of the knee, where it becomes the popliteal artery.
