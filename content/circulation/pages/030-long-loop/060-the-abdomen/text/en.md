Below the diaphragm the aorta sends three unpaired arteries to the gut. The first, the [celiac trunk](@celiac-trunk), is a stem about a centimetre long that splits into three: the [left gastric artery](@left-gastric-artery) to the stomach, the [common hepatic](@common-hepatic-artery) to the liver and the [splenic artery](@splenic-artery) to the spleen.

@image celiac-gray-2bd2a5

The [superior mesenteric artery](@superior-mesenteric-artery) feeds the whole small intestine and the large intestine as far as two thirds of the way along the transverse colon. The [inferior mesenteric](@inferior-mesenteric-artery) feeds the rest, down to the rectum.

@image superior-mesenteric-artery-gray-249fac

At rest the gut, the liver, the pancreas and the spleen receive about a quarter of the heart's output. After a meal the arterioles of the gut widen, and the flow rises further while the food is absorbed.
