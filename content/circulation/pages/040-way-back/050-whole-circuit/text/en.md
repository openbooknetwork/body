The two loops make one circuit, and the blood passes through the heart twice on each round: the right side sends it to the lungs, the left side to the body. [Arteries](@arteries) carry blood away from the heart and [veins](@veins) bring it back, whatever its oxygen. The [pulmonary arteries](@pulmonary-arteries) carry dark blood, the [pulmonary veins](@pulmonary-veins) bright.

At rest a red cell completes the round in about a minute. At any moment the veins hold about two thirds of the blood, the arteries about an eighth, and the arterioles and capillaries less than a tenth. The heart and the vessels of the lungs hold the rest.

Wide and slack, the veins act as a reservoir. When the body needs more output, their walls tighten and send part of their store to the heart.
