The veins gather what the arteries spread. From the head the [internal jugular veins](@internal-jugular-veins) and from the arms the [subclavian veins](@subclavian-veins) join into the two [brachiocephalic veins](@brachiocephalic-veins), and these into the [superior vena cava](@superior-vena-cava), which empties into the right atrium from above. From the legs the [great saphenous](@great-saphenous-veins), the longest vein in the body, and the deep [femoral veins](@femoral-veins) run up into the [iliac veins](@common-iliac-veins) and the [inferior vena cava](@inferior-vena-cava), which climbs beside the aorta, takes the [renal](@renal-veins) and [hepatic veins](@hepatic-veins), passes through the diaphragm and enters the atrium from below.

By the time it reaches the veins the blood has almost no pressure left. What pushes it up from the feet is the squeeze of the leg muscles on the deep veins, and what stops it falling back is the valves inside them, pockets every few centimetres that close under the weight of the column above.
