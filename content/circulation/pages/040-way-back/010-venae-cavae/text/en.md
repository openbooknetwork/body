The veins gather what the arteries spread. The [internal jugular veins](@internal-jugular-veins) from the head and the [subclavian veins](@subclavian-veins) from the arms join behind the collarbones into the two [brachiocephalic veins](@brachiocephalic-veins). These meet in the [superior vena cava](@superior-vena-cava), which empties into the right atrium from above.

The [inferior vena cava](@inferior-vena-cava), the widest vein in the body, begins where the two [common iliac veins](@common-iliac-veins) meet, at the fifth lumbar vertebra. It climbs to the right of the aorta, takes in the [renal](@renal-veins) and [hepatic veins](@hepatic-veins), passes through the diaphragm and enters the right atrium from below.

The [azygos vein](@azygos-vein) runs up beside the spine and collects blood from the walls of the chest. It arches over the root of the right lung into the superior vena cava, and gives blood a way round when the inferior vena cava is blocked.
