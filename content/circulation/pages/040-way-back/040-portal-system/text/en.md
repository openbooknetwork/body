Part of the venous blood takes a detour. The blood that leaves the gut, rich in what the meal contained, is gathered by the [superior](@superior-mesenteric-vein) and [inferior mesenteric veins](@inferior-mesenteric-vein) and the [splenic vein](@splenic-vein). Behind the pancreas they join into the [portal vein](@portal-vein), which carries the blood into the liver.

@image portal-vein-gray-939511

In the liver this blood spreads through a second set of capillaries. Liver cells take up sugars and store or release them, make proteins, and break down drugs and poisons. Then the blood collects in the [hepatic veins](@hepatic-veins) and joins the inferior vena cava just below the heart.

A vein that runs between two capillary beds is called a portal vein, and this is the largest. About three quarters of the liver's blood arrives this way, and a quarter by the hepatic artery.
