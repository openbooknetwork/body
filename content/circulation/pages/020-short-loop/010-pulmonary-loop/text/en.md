From the right ventricle the blood leaves by the [pulmonary trunk](@pulmonary-trunk), which divides after five centimetres into the [right](@right-pulmonary-artery) and [left pulmonary arteries](@left-pulmonary-artery), one to each lung. These are the only arteries that carry blood low in oxygen. Inside the lungs they branch with the airways down to capillaries that wrap the 300 million air sacs, where the blood gives up carbon dioxide and takes up oxygen through a wall a thousandth of a millimetre thick.

The capillaries gather into four [pulmonary veins](@pulmonary-veins), two from each lung, which open into the left atrium. This is the short loop. The lungs are close and their vessels wide, so the right ventricle drives it at a fifth of the pressure the left needs for the body.
