From the right ventricle the blood leaves by the [pulmonary trunk](@pulmonary-trunk). After about five centimetres it divides into the [right](@right-pulmonary-artery) and [left pulmonary arteries](@left-pulmonary-artery), one for each lung. These are the only arteries in an adult that carry blood low in oxygen.

@image chest-section-gray-f502ac

Inside each lung the arteries branch alongside the [airways](@bronchi), each bronchus with its artery, down to capillaries in the walls of some 300 million air sacs, the alveoli.

@image bronchiole-alveoli-f3da4c

The lungs lie close to the heart and their vessels are wide, so the right ventricle drives this loop with little pressure: about 25 millimetres of mercury at the peak of each beat, a fifth of what the left ventricle needs for the body.
