In the wall of each alveolus, blood and air are separated by less than a thousandth of a millimetre of tissue. Oxygen passes into the blood and carbon dioxide out. At rest a red cell spends about three quarters of a second in a lung capillary, and it is fully loaded within the first third of that time.

The capillaries of the lungs hold only 60 to 140 millilitres of blood at any moment, spread over some 70 square metres in a film one red cell thick.

They drain into veins that run between the segments of the lung and join into the [pulmonary veins](@pulmonary-veins), usually two from each lung. These carry the bright blood to the [left atrium](@left-atrium). In an adult they are the only veins that carry blood rich in oxygen.
