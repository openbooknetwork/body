With the first breaths the lungs fill with air, their vessels open, and the resistance to flow through them falls sharply. Blood now pours through the [pulmonary arteries](@pulmonary-arteries) and returns by the [pulmonary veins](@pulmonary-veins). The pressure in the left atrium rises above that in the right and presses the flap of the foramen ovale shut.

The [ductus arteriosus](@ductus-arteriosus) contracts within a few days of birth and turns into a fibrous cord, the ligamentum arteriosum, between the pulmonary trunk and the arch of the aorta. The flap of the foramen ovale grows onto the septum and leaves a shallow dip, the fossa ovalis, in the wall of the right atrium.

In about a quarter of adults the flap never grows fully onto the septum, and a small opening remains. It stays closed as long as the pressure in the left atrium is the higher, and most people who have one never know.
