Before birth the lungs are filled with fluid and do no breathing. Oxygen comes from the placenta, through the umbilical vein in the cord. Most of this blood bypasses the liver through a short channel, the ductus venosus, and reaches the [right atrium](@right-atrium) through the inferior vena cava.

Two shortcuts let most of the blood skip the lungs. A hole with a flap in the wall between the atria, the foramen ovale, lets blood pass from the right atrium straight into the [left atrium](@left-atrium). Most of the blood that does reach the pulmonary trunk is turned into the aorta through a short vessel, the [ductus arteriosus](@ductus-arteriosus).

The lungs, still full of fluid, resist the flow, so the pressure on the right side of the heart is higher than on the left. That keeps both shortcuts open, and only a small part of the blood passes through the lungs.
