Blood is made inside bone. In a child every bone holds red marrow; in an adult it survives in the [skull](@skull), the [sternum](@sternum) and ribs, the [vertebrae](@vertebral-column), the [pelvis](@pelvic-girdle) and the upper ends of the [femurs](@femurs), about a kilogram and a half in all, while the long shafts have turned to yellow fat. A sample for diagnosis is drawn from the hip bone.

The marrow turns out some two million red cells a second, 200 billion a day, together with the white cells and the platelets. A red cell takes about a week to mature, loses its nucleus on the way, and leaves the marrow through the thin walls of its sinuses as a disc of haemoglobin 7 micrometres across. The kidneys set the rate: when they sense too little oxygen in the blood passing through them, they release erythropoietin and the marrow speeds up, which is why people living at altitude have thicker blood.
