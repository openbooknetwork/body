The [spinal cord](@spinal-cord) runs down a canal through the vertebrae, from the base of the skull to the small of the back. It is about 45 centimetres long and as thick as a finger, and like the brain it is wrapped in three membranes. Cut across, it shows a butterfly of grey matter inside the white.

@image spinal-cord-section-gray-b6d6fd

Between each pair of vertebrae a nerve leaves on each side, 31 pairs in all: 8 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral and 1 coccygeal. Each nerve joins two roots. The [back root](@posterior-roots), with its swelling, the [spinal ganglion](@spinal-ganglia), brings sensation in. The [front root](@anterior-roots) sends commands out. Charles Bell and François Magendie showed this between 1811 and 1822.

@image spinal-nerve-roots-616678

The third, fourth and fifth cervical nerves form the phrenic nerve to the [diaphragm](@diaphragm). An injury to the cord above the third cervical segment stops breathing.
