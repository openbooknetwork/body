The [cord](@spinal-cord) grows more slowly than the spine. At birth it reaches the third lumbar vertebra, in an adult only the border between the [first](@first-lumbar-vertebra) and [second lumbar vertebrae](@second-lumbar-vertebra). There it tapers to a point. The nerve roots for the legs and pelvis run on down the canal to their exits, a bundle called the [cauda equina](@cauda-equina), the horse's tail.

Below the end of the cord a needle can enter the canal and miss it. In a lumbar puncture the doctor passes a fine needle between the [third](@third-lumbar-vertebra) and [fourth lumbar vertebrae](@fourth-lumbar-vertebra) and draws off a few millilitres of cerebrospinal fluid. Heinrich Quincke introduced the method in Kiel in 1891.
