The [sciatic nerve](@sciatic-nerves), about 2 centimetres wide, is the body's largest nerve. It gathers the fourth lumbar to third sacral nerves, leaves the pelvis through the buttock and runs down the back of the thigh. Above the knee it splits into the [tibial nerve](@tibial-nerves) for the calf and sole and the [common fibular nerve](@common-fibular-nerves) for the front of the leg.

The common fibular nerve winds round the neck of the fibula, where crossed legs or a tight plaster can press it and leave the foot dropping. The front of the thigh belongs to the [femoral nerve](@femoral-nerves). Sciatica, pain down the back of the leg, usually comes from a slipped disc pressing on the fifth lumbar or the first sacral root.
