Five spinal nerves, the fifth cervical to the first thoracic, mix in the neck and armpit as the brachial plexus. The five [roots](@brachial-plexus-roots) join into three [trunks](@brachial-plexus-trunks). Each trunk splits into a front and a back [division](@brachial-plexus-divisions), and the divisions regroup into three cords around the axillary artery. The back divisions together form the [posterior cord](@brachial-plexus-cords).

The cords end in five nerves: the musculocutaneous, axillary, radial, median and ulnar. Each arm muscle therefore receives fibres from more than one cord segment. A hard pull of the head away from the shoulder, as in a difficult birth, can tear the upper trunk and leave the arm hanging and turned in. Erb described this palsy in 1874.
