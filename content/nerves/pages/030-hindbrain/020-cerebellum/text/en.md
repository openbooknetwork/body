Under the back of the hemispheres sits the [cerebellum](@cerebellum), the little brain. It is about a tenth of the brain's weight, yet it holds about 69 of the brain's 86 billion nerve cells. Most of them are tiny granule cells packed into its thin, finely folded cortex.

The cerebellum compares the movement the cortex intends with the movement the body makes, and corrects it while it happens. Damage to it leaves strength intact but makes movements clumsy and shaky on the same side of the body, the walk wide and staggering, and speech slurred. Alcohol does much the same, which is why a drunk person staggers.
