Shine a light into one eye and both pupils narrow within a second. The signal runs from the retina to the midbrain and back out in the [oculomotor nerves](@oculomotor-nerves) to the [ciliary ganglion](@ciliary-ganglia), a parasympathetic relay 2 millimetres long behind the eye. From there the [short ciliary nerves](@short-ciliary-nerves) reach the ring muscle of the [iris](@iris).

In the dark, or in fright, sympathetic fibres from the upper chest segments of the cord pull the pupil wide through a second, radial muscle. The pupil ranges from about 1.5 to 8 millimetres. Doctors shine a torch into the eyes of an unconscious patient because the reflex tests the midbrain itself.

Tears are parasympathetic too: fibres of the facial nerve reach the [lacrimal gland](@lacrimal-glands) above the outer corner of the eye.
