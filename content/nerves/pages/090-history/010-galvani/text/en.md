In the 1780s Luigi Galvani, a professor of anatomy in Bologna, cut frogs down to their [lower spine](@lumbar-vertebrae) and legs, joined only by the [nerves of the legs](@sciatic-nerves). When a brass hook through the spinal cord touched an iron railing, the legs twitched. An arc of two metals laid from nerve to muscle did the same.

@image galvani-frog-legs-517bf7

In 1791 Galvani concluded that nerve and muscle carry an electricity of their own, which the metals only released. Alessandro Volta replied that the current came from the two metals, and to prove it he built the first battery in 1800. Both were partly right: the metals had made the current, and nerves do run on electricity.
