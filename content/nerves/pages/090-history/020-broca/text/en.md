In April 1861 Louis Victor Leborgne, aged 51, was moved to the surgical ward of the Bicêtre hospital in Paris, where Paul Broca examined him. For 21 years he had been able to say only one syllable, "tan", yet he understood what was said to him and answered with gestures. He died six days later.

At the autopsy Broca found a soft cavity in the back of the [third frontal convolution](@left-inferior-frontal-gyrus) of the left hemisphere. He showed the brain to the Anthropological Society the next day. After more cases he wrote in 1865 that "we speak with the left hemisphere". The brain is still kept in Paris.
