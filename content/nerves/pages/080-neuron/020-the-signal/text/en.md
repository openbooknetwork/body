At rest the inside of a nerve cell is 60 to 90 millivolts negative, held by pumps that move sodium out and potassium in. A stimulus past threshold opens sodium channels, sodium floods in and the inside swings to 30 or 40 millivolts positive. Within a millisecond potassium flows out and the voltage falls. This spike is the action potential.

The spike spreads by triggering the membrane next to it. Bare fibres carry it at as little as 0.25 metres a second. In fibres wrapped in myelin it jumps between gaps 1 to 3 millimetres apart, the nodes of Ranvier, at up to 100 metres a second. The [white matter](@white-matter) holds 150,000 to 180,000 kilometres of such fibres.

At the end of the axon the signal crosses to the next cell as a chemical. The spike opens calcium channels, and small vesicles release a transmitter into a cleft 20 to 30 nanometres wide. The crossing takes about half a millisecond.
