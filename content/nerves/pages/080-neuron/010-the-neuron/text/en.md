A nerve cell, or neuron, has three parts. The cell body holds the nucleus. Branching dendrites take in signals, through up to 200,000 contacts on a single motor neuron. One long axon sends the cell's own signal away, sometimes a metre. The grey matter of the [cortex](@cerebral-cortex) is packed with cell bodies and dendrites, the [white matter](@white-matter) with axons.

The brain holds about as many supporting glial cells as neurons, some 85 billion. Some wrap axons in myelin, some feed the neurons and clear away their waste, and some act as the brain's immune cells.
