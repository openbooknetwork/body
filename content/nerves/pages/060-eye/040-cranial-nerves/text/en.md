Twelve pairs of nerves leave the brain itself and pass out through holes in the skull. Numbered from front to back they are the [olfactory](@olfactory-nerves), [optic](@optic-nerves), [oculomotor](@oculomotor-nerves), [trochlear](@trochlear-nerves), [trigeminal](@trigeminal), [abducens](@abducens-nerves), [facial](@facial-nerves), [vestibulocochlear](@vestibulocochlear-nerves), [glossopharyngeal](@glossopharyngeal-nerves), [vagus](@vagus-nerves), [accessory](@accessory-nerves) and [hypoglossal](@hypoglossal-nerves). Some carry only sensation, some only commands to muscles, and several carry both.

The trigeminal nerve feels the face through three divisions. The [ophthalmic](@ophthalmic-nerves) reaches the forehead, upper lid, nose and cornea. The [maxillary](@maxillary-nerves) reaches the cheek and upper teeth. The [mandibular](@mandibular-nerves) reaches the jaw, lower teeth and front of the tongue, and works the chewing muscles.

@image trigeminal-gray-b0b57f

A touch on the cornea makes both eyes blink: the ophthalmic nerve carries the touch in and the facial nerve closes the lids.
