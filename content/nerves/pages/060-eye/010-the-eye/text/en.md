The eyeball is about 24 millimetres across. Light enters through the clear [cornea](@cornea), which does about two thirds of the focusing, passes the hole in the coloured [iris](@iris), the pupil, and is focused by the [lens](@lens) onto the [retina](@retina) at the back. Muscles round the lens let it round up for near things and flatten for far ones.

The retina is a thin sheet of brain pushed out into the eye during development. Its light-sensitive cells, about 100 million rods for dim light and 3 million cones for colour and detail, report through bipolar cells to 1.6 million ganglion cells. The axons of the ganglion cells form the optic nerve.
