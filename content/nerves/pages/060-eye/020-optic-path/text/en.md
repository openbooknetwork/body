The two [optic nerves](@optic-nerves) leave the back of the eyes and meet under the brain at the [optic chiasm](@optic-chiasm), just in front of the pituitary. There the fibres from the inner half of each retina cross to the other side. Each [optic tract](@optic-tracts) behind the chiasm therefore carries one half, left or right, of what both eyes see.

@image visual-pathway-gray-a1e990

Each tract ends in a relay of the thalamus, the [lateral geniculate body](@lateral-geniculate-bodies). From there a broad band of fibres, the optic radiation, fans back to the visual cortex in the [occipital lobe](@occipital-lobes). A stroke in the right occipital lobe blinds the left half of the field of view of both eyes.

In his Treatise of Man, printed in 1664, René Descartes drew the path as tubes from the eyes to the pineal gland, which he took for the seat of the soul.
