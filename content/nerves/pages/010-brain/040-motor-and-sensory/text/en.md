On either side of the central sulcus runs a strip of cortex that holds a map of the body. The [precentral gyrus](@precentral-gyri), in front, sends the commands that move the muscles. The [postcentral gyrus](@postcentral-gyri), behind, receives touch, pressure and pain. In both, the feet lie at the top near the midline and the face lies low on the side.

@image precentral-gyrus-gray-9c9731

The map is out of proportion. The hands, lips and tongue take far more cortex than the back or the thigh, because they move finely and feel finely. Drawn to that scale the body becomes the homunculus, the little man that Wilder Penfield and Edwin Boldrey published in 1937 after stimulating the cortex of awake patients during surgery for epilepsy.
