Two areas of the left hemisphere carry most of the work of language. Broca's area, in the back of the [inferior frontal gyrus](@left-inferior-frontal-gyrus), turns words into speech. A person with damage there understands, but speaks slowly in short, broken phrases.

Wernicke's area, at the back of the [superior temporal gyrus](@left-posterior-superior-temporal-gyrus) next to the hearing cortex, gives words their meaning. With damage there, speech flows but is empty or garbled, and the person does not follow what is said to them. A bundle of fibres, the arcuate fasciculus, links the two areas.

Carl Wernicke described the second area in 1874, at the age of 26, from patients who spoke fluently and made no sense.
