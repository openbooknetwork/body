Cut down the middle, the brain shows a white arch between the halves, the [corpus callosum](@corpus-callosum). It is about 10 centimetres long and carries some 200 million nerve fibres from each hemisphere to the matching place in the other, so that the two halves work as one brain.

@image brain-median-section-gray-664bfa

In the 1960s surgeons cut it in a few people whose epileptic seizures spread from one half to the other. Roger Sperry tested them. A word shown only to the right hemisphere could not be spoken, since speech lives in the left, yet the left hand could pick out the object it named. Sperry shared the Nobel Prize in 1981.
