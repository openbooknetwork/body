Deep in each hemisphere lie clusters of grey matter called the basal ganglia. The [caudate nucleus](@caudate-nuclei) curls like a tail beside the lateral ventricle. The [putamen](@putamen) lies further out, and inside it sits the [globus pallidus](@globus-pallidus), the pale globe. Loops run from the cortex through these nuclei and the thalamus back to the cortex.

The loops select which movement to make and hold back the rest. They depend on dopamine from a small dark nucleus of the midbrain, the substantia nigra. When its cells die, movements grow slow and small and the hands shake at rest. James Parkinson described this shaking palsy in 1817.
