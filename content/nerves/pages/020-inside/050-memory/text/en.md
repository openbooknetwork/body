Curled in the floor of each temporal lobe lies the [hippocampus](@hippocampi), named for its seahorse shape. It turns the events of the day into lasting memories, which then settle across the cortex. Its output runs forward in an arching bundle, the [fornix](@fornix). In front of it sits the [amygdala](@amygdalae), an almond of grey matter that tags events with fear.

In 1953 the surgeon William Scoville removed most of the hippocampus and the amygdala on both sides from Henry Molaison, aged 27, to stop his seizures. The seizures eased, but from then on he could not form a new lasting memory. He kept his old memories and learned new skills, such as drawing in a mirror, without remembering the practice.
