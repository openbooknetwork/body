Cut across, the brain shows two shades. The grey matter, the [cortex](@cerebral-cortex) on the surface and the nuclei deep inside, is where the bodies of the nerve cells sit and their connections are made. The [white matter](@white-matter) under the cortex is cabling: billions of nerve fibres wrapped in fatty myelin, which gives it its colour.

@image brain-coronal-slice-048f82

The fibres run three ways. Some join areas within one hemisphere, some cross to the other hemisphere, and some run between the cortex and the brainstem and cord. These gather into a fan, the [internal capsule](@internal-capsules), which passes between the deep nuclei. A small stroke there can paralyse a whole half of the body.
