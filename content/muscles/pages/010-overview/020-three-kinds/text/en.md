Muscle tissue comes in three kinds. Skeletal muscle, such as the [biceps](@biceps-brachii) of the arm, moves the bones and obeys the will. Its cells are long fibres with many nuclei, and under the microscope they show fine cross stripes, so it is also called striated muscle.

Cardiac muscle forms the wall of the [heart](@heart). It is striated too, but its cells are short and branched and joined end to end by intercalated discs, which pass the signal from cell to cell. It contracts in its own rhythm, about 70 times a minute at rest, and keeps working throughout life.

Smooth muscle lines the walls of hollow organs such as the [stomach](@stomach), the intestines, the blood vessels, the airways and the bladder. Its spindle-shaped cells have no stripes. It contracts slowly and can stay contracted for a long time on little energy. Cardiac and smooth muscle work without conscious control.
