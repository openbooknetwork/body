The [tibialis anterior](@tibialis-anterior) lies just outside the sharp front edge of the tibia. Its tendon crosses the front of the ankle to the inner side of the foot. It lifts the front of the foot so that the toes clear the ground as the leg swings forward in walking, and it lowers the foot gently after the heel strikes.

@image leg-front-gray-97a4e4

Beside it the [toe extensors](@toe-extensors) lift the toes, and bands of fascia, the [retinacula](@ankle-retinacula), hold their tendons down at the ankle like straps. On the outer side the [fibularis longus and brevis](@fibularis) turn the sole outward and steady the ankle. The tendon of the longus crosses under the sole and helps hold up the arch.

@image ankle-tendons-gray-44b04c

These muscles are supplied by branches of the common fibular nerve, which winds round the neck of the fibula just under the skin. If it is damaged there, the foot hangs down and the toes drag, so the person lifts the knee high with each step. This is called foot drop.
