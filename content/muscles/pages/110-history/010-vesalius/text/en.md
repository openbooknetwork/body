In 1543 Andreas Vesalius, a 28-year-old professor of anatomy at Padua, published De humani corporis fabrica, On the Fabric of the Human Body. He based it on human bodies he had dissected himself. Teaching until then followed the Greek physician Galen, who had dissected apes and other animals, and Vesalius showed where Galen's descriptions did not fit the human body.

The second of its seven books covers the muscles. Its full-page woodcuts show a man standing in a landscape, with a layer of muscle taken away on each plate until the bones show. Set side by side, the backgrounds join into one view of the hills near Padua. The drawings are usually credited to artists from Titian's workshop in Venice.
