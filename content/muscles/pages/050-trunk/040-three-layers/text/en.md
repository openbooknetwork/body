Under the external oblique lie two more flat muscles, shown here on opposite sides. The [internal oblique](@internal-oblique) runs at right angles to the external, up and forward from the hip bone and the lower back. Under it the [transversus abdominis](@transversus-abdominis) runs straight round the abdomen like a belt.

@image internal-oblique-gray-bc8ddb

Three layers with fibres in different directions make a wall that resists strain in every direction, as plywood does. In front, the aponeuroses of all three wrap around the [rectus abdominis](@rectus-abdominis) as its sheath and meet those of the other side at the linea alba.

The transversus is the deepest. Its contraction pulls the wall in and raises the pressure inside the abdomen. It tightens just before the arms or legs move, which steadies the lower back.
