Between the ribs and the pelvis the wall of the abdomen is made of muscle. The [rectus abdominis](@rectus-abdominis) runs as a strap on each side of the midline, from the pubis to the cartilages of the fifth to seventh ribs. Usually three tendinous bands cross each strap, which gives a lean abdomen its six bulges.

@image abdomen-muscles-openstax-93ae3f

On the sides the [external oblique](@external-oblique) starts on the lower eight ribs. Its fibres run down and forward, like fingers in the front pockets, and end in a flat sheet of tendon, the aponeurosis. It covers the rectus, so the model shows it on the right only. The sheets of both sides meet at the [linea alba](@linea-alba).

@image external-oblique-gray-586c8c

These muscles bend the trunk forward and twist it, and they press on the organs inside. That pressure helps in coughing, vomiting, giving birth and passing stool, and it steadies the spine in lifting.
