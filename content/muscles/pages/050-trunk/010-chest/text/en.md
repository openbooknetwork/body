The [pectoralis major](@pectoralis-major) covers the front of the chest. Its upper part starts on the clavicle and its larger lower part on the sternum and the upper ribs. The fibres converge on a flat tendon that twists on itself before it inserts on the humerus, below the shoulder. It pulls the arm forward and across the chest and turns it inward.

The [serratus anterior](@serratus-anterior) lies on the side of the chest. It starts in finger-like slips on the upper eight or nine ribs, which give it a saw-toothed edge, and passes behind the scapula to its inner border. It holds the scapula against the ribs and rotates it so that the arm can be raised above the head.

@image pectoral-girdle-openstax-ec4b77

If its nerve, the long thoracic nerve, is damaged, the inner edge of the scapula lifts away from the chest when the person pushes against a wall. This is called a winged scapula.
