Muscle fibres come in two types. Slow fibres, type I, contract slowly, burn fat and sugar with oxygen and work for hours without tiring. They are rich in mitochondria, capillaries and myoglobin, a red protein that stores oxygen, so they look red. Fast fibres, type II, contract faster and more strongly, rely more on sugar burned without oxygen and tire quickly.

Every muscle has both, in proportions that suit its work. The [soleus](@soleus), which holds the body upright all day, is over 80 percent slow fibres. The [gastrocnemius](@gastrocnemius), used for pushing off and jumping, is about half slow. The [tibialis anterior](@tibialis-anterior) is about 70 percent slow.

The mix differs between people and is mostly inherited. In the leg muscles of marathon runners about 80 percent of the fibres are slow, in sprinters fewer than 40 percent. In a chicken the leg muscles, used all day for walking, are dark meat, and the breast muscles, used in short bursts of flapping, are white.
