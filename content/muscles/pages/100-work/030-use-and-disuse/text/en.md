Heavy loads make muscle fibres thicker as they add actin and myosin filaments, while the number of fibres stays about the same. A few contractions a day above half of the maximal force build strength faster than many light ones. Strength rises about 30 percent in the first 6 to 8 weeks of training, then levels off.

Without use a muscle wastes, because its proteins are broken down faster than they are made. The muscles that hold the body up against gravity, in the [back](@erector-spinae), [buttocks](@gluteus-maximus), [thighs](@quadriceps) and [calves](@triceps-surae), shrink fastest in bed rest or in space.

A muscle also adapts its length. Kept stretched, it adds sarcomeres at the ends of its fibres. Kept short, as in a plaster cast, it loses some, and it takes stretching to bring the length back.
