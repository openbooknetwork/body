The shoulder joint is a large ball on a small, shallow socket, which lets the arm move further than at any other joint. Four muscles of the scapula hold the ball in the socket. Their tendons blend with the joint capsule and wrap round it like a cuff, the rotator cuff.

@image scapula-back-gray-35e2c7

The [supraspinatus](@supraspinatus) lies above the spine of the scapula and helps raise the arm. The [infraspinatus](@infraspinatus) and the [teres minor](@teres-minor) below the spine turn the arm outward. The [subscapularis](@subscapularis) lies on the front of the scapula, against the ribs, and turns the arm inward.

@image humerus-muscles-openstax-81f9e7

The supraspinatus tendon passes through a narrow gap between the head of the humerus and the acromion. There it can be pinched when the arm is raised, and with age it wears. Tears of the rotator cuff become common after middle age, and most involve this tendon.
