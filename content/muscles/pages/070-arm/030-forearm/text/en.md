The forearm holds 20 muscles, most of them long and thin. The [wrist flexors](@wrist-flexors) lie on the palm side and start together on the medial epicondyle, the bump on the inner side of the elbow. With the finger flexors beneath them they bend the wrist and fingers, and one of them, the pronator teres, turns the palm down.

The [wrist extensors](@wrist-extensors) and the [finger extensors](@finger-extensors) lie on the back and start on the lateral epicondyle, on the outer side of the elbow. They straighten the wrist and fingers. Overuse of their common tendon in gripping causes tennis elbow, a pain over that bump.

@image forearm-back-gray-0d7098

The [brachioradialis](@brachioradialis) runs from the lower humerus to the end of the radius at the wrist. It bends the elbow and is strongest with the thumb pointing up, as when lifting a glass.
