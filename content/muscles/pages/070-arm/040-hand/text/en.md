The muscles that bend the fingers most strongly sit in the forearm and reach the fingers by long tendons, which keeps the fingers slim. The [flexor digitorum superficialis](@flexor-digitorum-superficialis) sends a tendon to the middle bone of each finger. The [flexor digitorum profundus](@deep-finger-flexors) beneath it sends its tendons through splits in those tendons to the fingertips.

At the wrist these eight tendons and the long flexor tendon of the thumb pass through the carpal tunnel, a channel between the wrist bones and a band across them, the flexor retinaculum. The median nerve goes through with them. Swollen tendon sheaths can press on it and cause carpal tunnel syndrome, with tingling in the thumb and first fingers.

About 20 small muscles lie inside the hand itself. The [thenar muscles](@thenar-muscles) form the ball of the thumb, the [hypothenar muscles](@hypothenar-muscles) the edge of the palm by the little finger, and the interossei and lumbricals lie between the bones. They make the fine movements of the fingers, and the story of the hand covers them.
