Two broad, flat muscles cover most of the back. The [trapezius](@trapezius) spreads over the neck and upper back, and the right and left muscles together form a large four-sided figure, the trapezium that gives the muscle its name. Below it the [latissimus dorsi](@latissimus-dorsi), the widest muscle of the back, covers the lower back and the side of the chest.

The latissimus dorsi starts on the spines of the lower six thoracic vertebrae, on the lumbar vertebrae and sacrum through the thoracolumbar fascia, and on the iliac crest. Its fibres narrow into a tendon only a few centimetres wide, which winds round the lower edge of the armpit to the front of the humerus.

It pulls the raised arm down and back and turns it inward, as in climbing, rowing, swimming and pull-ups. When the arms are fixed, as on crutches or on parallel bars, it lifts the trunk.
