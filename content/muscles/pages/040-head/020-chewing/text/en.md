Four pairs of muscles move the lower jaw in chewing. The [masseter](@masseter) runs from the cheekbone to the angle of the jaw and can be felt hardening when the teeth clench. The [temporalis](@temporalis) fans out over the side of the skull and narrows to a tendon on the jaw's coronoid process. Both close the mouth.

Two smaller pairs, the medial and lateral pterygoids, lie deep to the jaw. They pull it forward and from side to side, so that the molars grind the food. All four pairs are supplied by the mandibular branch of the trigeminal nerve.

The jaw muscles can press the incisors together with a force of about 25 kilograms and the molars with about 90 kilograms.
