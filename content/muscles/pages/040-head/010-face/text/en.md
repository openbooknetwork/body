The muscles of the face are thin sheets that start on bone and end in the skin, so when they contract they move the skin. There are about 20 on each side, all supplied by the facial nerve. They close the eyes and lips, help with speech and eating, and make the expressions people read on a face.

The [orbicularis oculi](@orbicularis-oculi) is a ring around the eye that closes the lids, gently in a blink and tightly in a squint. The [orbicularis oris](@orbicularis-oris) rings the mouth and closes and purses the lips. The [frontalis](@frontalis) raises the eyebrows and wrinkles the forehead. The [zygomaticus major](@zygomaticus-major) pulls the corner of the mouth up and out in a smile.

When the facial nerve stops working on one side, as in Bell's palsy, that half of the face hangs still. The eye stays open and the corner of the mouth droops.
