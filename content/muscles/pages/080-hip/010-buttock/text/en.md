The [gluteus maximus](@gluteus-maximus) is the heaviest muscle in the body and gives the buttock its shape. It starts on the back of the ilium, the sacrum and the coccyx, and runs down and out to the femur and to the iliotibial tract, a band of tendon down the outside of the thigh.

@image gluteal-hamstrings-gray-1e77f6

It straightens the hip with force in climbing stairs, running, jumping and rising from a chair, and does little in walking on level ground. Humans have a far larger gluteus maximus than apes, and the muscle works hardest in running.

The [gluteus medius](@gluteus-medius) lies above and partly under it, on the outer side of the hip bone, and ends on the greater trochanter of the femur. In walking it holds the pelvis level each time the body stands on one leg. When it is weak, the pelvis drops on the side of the lifted leg.
