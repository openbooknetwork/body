The strongest muscle that bends the hip lies inside the abdomen and pelvis. The [psoas major](@psoas-major) starts on the sides of the lumbar vertebrae and the last thoracic vertebra. The [iliacus](@iliacus) fills the hollow on the inner side of the hip bone. Together they form the iliopsoas.

@image abdomen-muscles-openstax-93ae3f

The two pass under the inguinal ligament into the thigh and end on one tendon on the lesser trochanter of the femur. The iliopsoas lifts the thigh forward in walking, running and climbing. When the legs are fixed, as in a sit-up, it bends the trunk forward at the hips.

@image thigh-front-gray-f6e68c

The psoas lies against the back wall of the abdomen. Inflammation of an organ next to it, such as the appendix on the right, can make it painful to stretch the hip backward. Doctors test for this as the psoas sign.
