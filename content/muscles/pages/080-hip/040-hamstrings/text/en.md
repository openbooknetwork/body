The [hamstrings](@hamstrings) are three muscles on the back of the thigh. The [semitendinosus](@semitendinosus) and the [semimembranosus](@semimembranosus) run down the inner side to the tibia, the [biceps femoris](@biceps-femoris) down the outer side to the head of the fibula. Their tendons can be felt as cords on both sides of the back of the bent knee.

@image gluteal-hamstrings-gray-1e77f6

All three start on the ischial tuberosity, the bone a person sits on, apart from the short head of the biceps, which starts on the femur. They cross both the hip and the knee, so they straighten the hip and bend the knee. In walking they slow the swinging leg just before the heel strikes the ground.

@image hip-thigh-muscles-openstax-40c4f2

Sprinters often strain a hamstring at the moment the muscle is stretched while it contracts hard. All three are supplied by the sciatic nerve. The name joins ham, an old word for the back of the thigh and knee, with string, for the tendons.
