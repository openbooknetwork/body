The adductors fill the inner side of the thigh. The [pectineus](@pectineus), the [adductor longus](@adductor-longus), the [adductor brevis](@adductor-brevis) and the large [adductor magnus](@adductor-magnus) run from the pubis and ischium to the back of the femur. The long, thin [gracilis](@gracilis) runs on to the tibia.

@image adductors-gray-dc838a

They pull the thigh toward the midline, press the knees together and steady the pelvis in walking. Riders use them to grip the horse. A sudden stretch, as in a sliding tackle, can tear them, the groin strain of footballers. Most of them are supplied by the obturator nerve.

The [sartorius](@sartorius) crosses over them, from the front of the hip bone down across the thigh to the inner side of the tibia. It is the longest muscle in the body. It helps bring the leg into the cross-legged seat of a tailor, and its name comes from sartor, Latin for tailor.
