A muscle fibre is packed with hundreds to thousands of myofibrils, threads about one micrometre thick that run its whole length. Each myofibril is a chain of short units, the sarcomeres, joined end to end at thin plates called Z discs. In the [biceps](@biceps-brachii) a single myofibril holds tens of thousands of sarcomeres in a row.

A sarcomere is about 2 micrometres long. Thin filaments of the protein actin reach in from each Z disc. Between them lie thick filaments of myosin, about 1.6 micrometres long. The length of the myosin looks dark, the A band. Near the Z discs, where there is actin alone, the sarcomere looks light, the I band.

The bands of neighbouring myofibrils line up across the fibre. This gives skeletal and cardiac muscle the stripes seen under the microscope.
