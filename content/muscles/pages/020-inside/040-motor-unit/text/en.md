Each fibre takes its orders from one motor neuron in the spinal cord. The neuron's axon branches in the muscle, and each branch ends on one fibre at a neuromuscular junction. There the nerve ending releases acetylcholine, which starts an impulse in the fibre. One neuron and all the fibres it supplies form a motor unit.

The [musculocutaneous nerve](@musculocutaneous-nerves), from the fifth and sixth cervical segments, supplies the [coracobrachialis](@coracobrachialis), the [biceps](@biceps-brachii) and the [brachialis](@brachialis). Muscles that need fine control have small units: in some muscles of the larynx one neuron supplies two or three fibres. In the soleus of the calf one neuron supplies several hundred.

A unit contracts fully or not at all. The nervous system grades force by calling on more units, small ones first, and by firing each one faster. Impulses that come fast enough fuse the separate twitches into one smooth, steady contraction.
