This cut through the upper arm shows the [biceps](@biceps-brachii) and the [brachialis](@brachialis) in front of the [humerus](@humeri) and the [triceps](@triceps-brachii) behind it. Each muscle is wrapped in a sheath of connective tissue, the epimysium, which runs on into its tendons.

@image muscle-structure-seer-bec170

Inside, thinner sheets divide the muscle into bundles called fascicles, the grain seen in a cut of meat. Each fascicle holds many muscle fibres. A fibre is a single cell 10 to 80 micrometres thick with many nuclei, and in most muscles it runs the whole length of the muscle.

Blood vessels and nerves run in the connective tissue between the fascicles, and every fibre lies close to capillaries. At each end the connective tissue gathers into the tendon, so the pull of all the fibres adds up on the bone.
