Each bone has a dense outer shell of compact bone. Inside, especially at the ends, lies spongy bone, a lattice of thin struts and plates called trabeculae. In a long bone such as the [femur](@right-femur), the shaft is a tube of compact bone around a cavity that holds marrow. About four fifths of the skeleton's weight is compact bone.

The trabeculae line up along the forces a bone carries. In the head and neck of the femur they run in arches, like the struts of a crane, so the bone is strong where it needs to be and light elsewhere.

@image long-bone-openstax-3c6cdb

By weight, bone is about 60 percent mineral, crystals of calcium phosphate, and the rest mostly collagen fibres and water. The mineral makes it hard. The collagen lets it bend a little without snapping.
