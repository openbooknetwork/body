With age the outer ring of a disc dries and can tear. The nucleus can then bulge or push out through the tear, which is called a herniated or slipped disc. In the lower back this most often happens at the [disc](@disc-l4-l5) between the fourth and fifth lumbar vertebrae, shown cut across here, or at the one below it.

@image herniated-disc-blausen-884faa

The [nucleus](@nucleus-l4-l5) usually breaks out backward and to one side, beside the [posterior longitudinal ligament](@posterior-longitudinal-ligament), which runs down the backs of the discs and is narrow in the lower back. There it presses on the nerve roots of the [cauda equina](@cauda-equina) where they leave the canal.

Pressure on these roots causes sciatica, pain that runs from the buttock down the back of the leg, sometimes with numbness or weakness. In most people it eases within weeks to a few months without surgery.
