The vertebral column of a child has 33 vertebrae: 7 [cervical](@cervical-vertebrae) in the neck, 12 [thoracic](@thoracic-vertebrae) that carry the ribs, 5 [lumbar](@lumbar-vertebrae) in the small of the back, 5 sacral and usually 4 coccygeal. In an adult the sacral ones have fused into the [sacrum](@sacrum) and the coccygeal ones into the coccyx, leaving 26 bones.

The vertebrae grow larger from the neck down, since each carries the weight above it. The column is about 70 cm long in a man and 60 cm in a woman. It shields the spinal cord and lets the trunk bend and turn. This model shows the coccyx joined to the sacrum.

The top two vertebrae carry the head. The [atlas](@atlas) holds the skull and lets it nod. The [axis](@axis) has a peg that rises into the ring of the atlas, and the atlas turns around it when you shake your head.
