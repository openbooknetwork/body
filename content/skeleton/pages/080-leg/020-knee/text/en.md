The knee joins the femur to the tibia. It is the largest joint in the body and works mainly as a hinge, with a little turning when it is bent. The rounded condyles of the femur rest on the flat top of the tibia, and two C-shaped wedges of fibrous cartilage, the [menisci](@right-menisci), deepen the fit and spread the load.

@image knee-menisci-gray-4e7cb3

Inside the joint the [anterior cruciate ligament](@right-anterior-cruciate-ligament) and the [posterior cruciate ligament](@right-posterior-cruciate-ligament) cross each other. The anterior one stops the tibia sliding forward under the femur, the posterior one stops it sliding back. The [collateral ligaments](@right-knee-collateral-ligaments) on each side keep the knee from bending sideways.

@image knee-cruciates-gray-000ace

In front, the [patella](@right-patella), the kneecap, lies in the tendon of the quadriceps, the large muscle at the front of the thigh. Holding the tendon away from the joint, it gives the muscle more leverage to straighten the knee.
