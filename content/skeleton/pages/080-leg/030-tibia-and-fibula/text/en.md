The lower leg has two bones. The [tibia](@right-tibia), the shin bone, carries the body's weight from the knee to the ankle. Its front edge lies just under the skin, which is why a knock on the shin hurts so much. The slender [fibula](@right-fibula) on the outer side carries little weight, but many muscles attach to it.

The two are bound along their length by the [interosseous membrane](@right-leg-membrane) and at the bottom by strong ligaments. Their lower ends, the knobs on either side of the ankle, grip the talus of the foot like a fork. The ankle joint hinges here, tilting the foot up and down.

The [ankle ligaments](@right-ankle-ligaments) hold the talus in this fork. Rolling the foot onto its outer edge strains the outer ones, and a sprained ankle usually means a torn anterior talofibular ligament, the one at the front.
