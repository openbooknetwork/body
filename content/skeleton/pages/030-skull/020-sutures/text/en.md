From the side the cranium shows a few large plates: the [frontal bone](@frontal-bone) of the forehead, the two [parietal bones](@parietal-bones) of the roof, the [occipital bone](@occipital-bone) at the back, a [temporal bone](@temporal-bones) over each ear and the [sphenoid](@sphenoid-bone), which crosses the base of the skull from side to side.

@image skull-side-gray-060ee8

The plates meet at sutures, wavy joints filled with a thin layer of fibrous tissue. The coronal suture crosses the top of the head between the frontal and parietal bones. The sagittal suture runs between the two parietal bones, and the lambdoid suture between them and the occipital bone.

The sutures let the skull grow with the brain. In adults they slowly fill with bone, at ages that vary widely. If a suture closes too early in a baby, a condition called craniosynostosis, the skull grows out of shape.
