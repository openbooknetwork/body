At birth the bones of the skull roof have not yet joined. Between them lie fontanelles, patches of membrane that can be felt as soft spots. The largest, the anterior fontanelle, is a diamond between the [frontal bone](@frontal-bone) and the two [parietal bones](@parietal-bones). The posterior fontanelle lies between the parietal bones and the [occipital bone](@occipital-bone).

The loose plates can slide over each other, so the head narrows a little as it passes through the birth canal. They also leave room for the brain, which more than doubles in weight in the first year.

@image newborn-skull-side-gray-9504a4

Smaller fontanelles lie at the sides of the skull. The posterior fontanelle closes within two or three months of birth. The anterior one usually closes during the second year.
