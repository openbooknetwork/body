The sternum, the breastbone, is a flat bone about 17 cm long in the front of the chest. It has three parts: the [manubrium](@manubrium) at the top, which carries the clavicles and the first ribs, the long [body](@sternum-body), and the small [xiphoid process](@xiphoid-process) at the bottom. The xiphoid is cartilage in youth and turns to bone in middle age.

The manubrium meets the body at a slight angle, which you can feel as a ridge. The second ribs join the sternum there, so doctors count the ribs from it. The [costal cartilages](@costal-cartilages) let the ribs spring as the chest moves, and they tend to calcify with age.

The sternum lies over the heart. In resuscitation, pressing it down 5 to 6 cm, 100 to 120 times a minute, squeezes blood out of the heart and keeps it flowing to the brain.
