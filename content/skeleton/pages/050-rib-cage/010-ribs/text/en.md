Twelve pairs of ribs curve forward from the thoracic vertebrae. The upper seven, the [true ribs](@true-ribs), each reach the sternum by their own costal cartilage. The [false ribs](@false-ribs), the eighth to tenth, join the cartilage of the rib above, and their cartilages are missing in this model. The last two, the [floating ribs](@floating-ribs), end free in the flank.

At the back each rib forms two joints with the spine, one with the vertebral bodies and one with a transverse process. Breathing in, the muscles between the ribs lift them, and they swing up and out like bucket handles, widening the chest around the [lungs](@lungs).

The cage shields the heart and lungs and, under its lower edge, the liver and spleen. Some people have a short extra rib above the first, a cervical rib, which can press on the nerves and artery of the arm.
