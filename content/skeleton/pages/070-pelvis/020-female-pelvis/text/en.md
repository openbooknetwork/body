The [pelvis](@pelvis) differs more between men and women than any other part of the skeleton. This model's pelvis is a man's. Its bones are heavy, and its inlet, the ring at the brim seen here from above, is narrow and heart-shaped.

@image male-pelvis-gray-8e4925

A woman's pelvis is wider and shallower, with lighter bones. The inlet is round or oval and wider from side to side. The angle under the [pubic symphysis](@pubic-symphysis) is about 80 to 85 degrees against 50 to 60 in a man, and the sacrum is shorter and broader. These differences give a baby's head room to pass at birth.

@image female-pelvis-gray-028455

Because the differences are so clear, the pelvis is the part of a skeleton that forensic scientists look at first to tell its sex.
