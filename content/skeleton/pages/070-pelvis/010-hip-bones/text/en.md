The pelvis is a ring of bone. Its sides and front are the two [hip bones](@hip-bones). At the back they meet the [sacrum](@sacrum) at the sacroiliac joints, which barely move and are held by some of the strongest ligaments in the body. In front they meet at the [pubic symphysis](@pubic-symphysis), a joint with a pad of fibrous cartilage.

@image hip-bone-gray-b02e0a

Each hip bone forms from three bones, the ilium above, the ischium below and behind, and the pubis in front. They meet at a Y-shaped cartilage in the acetabulum, the socket for the femur, and fuse into one bone in the teens. You feel the crest of the ilium at the waist and sit on the ischia.

The pelvis passes the weight of the trunk to the legs. It also cradles the bladder, the end of the bowel and the reproductive organs.
