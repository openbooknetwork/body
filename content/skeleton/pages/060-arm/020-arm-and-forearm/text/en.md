The [humerus](@right-humerus) is the bone of the upper arm. Its round head fits the socket of the scapula. At the elbow its lower end meets the two bones of the forearm: the [ulna](@right-ulna) on the side of the little finger and the [radius](@right-radius) on the side of the thumb.

@image humerus-gray-6d3352

The ulna grips the end of the humerus like a wrench round a bolt, and the elbow bends and straightens on this hinge. The radius turns. When you turn the palm down, the radius crosses over the ulna while its head spins in place at the elbow. A sheet of fibres, the [interosseous membrane](@right-forearm-membrane), binds the two shafts.

@image forearm-bones-gray-0481bc

The funny bone is the ulnar nerve, which runs in a groove behind the inner knob of the humerus. A knock there presses the nerve against the bone and sends a tingle to the little finger.
