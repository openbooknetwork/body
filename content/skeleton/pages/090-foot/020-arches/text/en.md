The bones of the foot form arches. The long arch on the inner side, seen here, runs from the heel through the talus and the navicular to the first three metatarsals. A lower arch runs along the outer side, and a third arch crosses the foot. Standing, the weight rests on the heel and the ball of the foot.

@image foot-medial-gray-4bdf72

Bone shapes, ligaments and muscles hold the arches up. The [spring ligament](@right-spring-ligament) runs from the calcaneus to the navicular and carries the head of the talus. The [long plantar ligament](@right-long-plantar-ligament) ties the calcaneus to the cuboid and the metatarsals. Under them the plantar fascia, a thick band, ties the heel to the toes like a bowstring.

At each step the arch flattens a little as the weight comes on and stiffens as the heel lifts. In a flat foot the inner arch is low or missing, which in most people causes no trouble.
