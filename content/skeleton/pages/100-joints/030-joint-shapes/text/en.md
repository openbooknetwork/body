The shapes of the bone ends decide how a synovial joint moves. In a ball and socket joint, such as the [shoulder](@right-shoulder-capsule) and the hip, a round head turns in a cup and can swing in every direction. The shoulder's cup is shallow, so it moves more than any other joint and dislocates more often.

A hinge joint moves in one plane, like a door. The [elbow](@right-elbow-capsule) is one, and so are the joints of the fingers. In a pivot joint a bone turns about its own axis. The head of the radius spins in a [ring of ligament](@right-annular-ligament) at the elbow, and the atlas turns on the peg of the axis.

Other shapes allow other movements. The saddle-shaped joint at the base of the thumb lets it swing across the palm, and the small bones of the wrist and foot glide on flat faces.
