A joint is a place where bones meet, and anatomists sort joints by what holds the bones together. In fibrous joints it is dense fibrous tissue, and the bones barely move. The sutures of the skull are fibrous joints, and so is the lower joint between the tibia and the fibula.

@image skull-side-gray-060ee8

In cartilaginous joints a pad of cartilage joins the bones and lets them move a little. The [intervertebral discs](@discs) and the [pubic symphysis](@pubic-symphysis) join bones in this way.

@image lumbar-disc-section-gray-1ac3d8

Most joints of the limbs are synovial joints. The ends of the bones are free to move on each other inside a capsule of fibrous tissue, as in the [shoulder](@shoulder-capsules), the [elbow](@elbow-capsules), the [hip](@hip-capsules) and the [knee](@knee-capsules).
