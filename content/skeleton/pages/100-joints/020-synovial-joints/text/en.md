In a synovial joint the bone ends are capped with smooth cartilage a few millimetres thick and closed in a [capsule](@right-hip-capsule). The capsule is lined by the synovial membrane, which makes synovial fluid, a slippery liquid like egg white. The fluid lets the cartilage slide and feeds it, since cartilage has no blood vessels.

The hip is a deep synovial joint, shown here by its lit capsule. The head of the [femur](@right-femur) sits in the acetabulum of the [hip bone](@right-hip-bone), deepened by a rim of fibrous cartilage, the [labrum](@right-acetabular-labrum). A small [ligament](@right-femoral-head-ligament) runs from the head to the floor of the socket.

Ligaments thicken the capsule. The iliofemoral ligament across the front of the hip is one of the strongest in the body. Standing, it is pulled tight and stops the hip bending backward.
