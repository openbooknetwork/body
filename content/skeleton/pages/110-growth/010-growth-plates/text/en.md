Most bones begin in the embryo as models of cartilage. From about the seventh week, bone starts to replace the cartilage in the middle of each shaft and spreads toward the ends. The flat bones of the skull roof form in a different way, directly in a sheet of membrane.

@image femur-ossification-gray-0701d4

Later, bone also starts to form in the ends. Between each end and the shaft a thin disc of cartilage stays, the growth plate. Its cells divide and bone replaces them on the shaft side, so the bone lengthens. The plates at the knee ends of the [femur](@right-femur) and the [tibia](@right-tibia) add most of the leg's length.

At the end of puberty, a few years earlier in girls than in boys, the plates turn to bone and growth in height stops. The last plate to close, at the inner end of the clavicle, closes in the mid twenties.
