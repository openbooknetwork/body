Bone mass peaks around the age of 30. After that, removal slowly outpaces building. In women the loss speeds up for several years after the menopause, when oestrogen falls. In osteoporosis bone has thinned so far that it breaks in a minor fall.

Spongy bone suffers most: its struts thin and break and the spaces widen. The typical breaks are in the bodies of the [vertebrae](@vertebral-column), the neck of the [femur](@right-femur) and the lower end of the [radius](@right-radius). Collapsed vertebrae make a person shorter and can bend the upper back forward.

Osteoporosis causes no symptoms until a bone breaks, but an X-ray scan of bone density can find it before. Exercise that loads the bones, enough calcium and vitamin D, and not smoking help keep bone, and medicines can slow the loss.
