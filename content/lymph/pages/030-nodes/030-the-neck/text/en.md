Every lymph vessel of the head and neck ends in the [deep cervical nodes](@neck-nodes). They form a chain along the [internal jugular vein](@internal-jugular-veins), from the base of the skull to the root of the neck, beside the [common carotid artery](@common-carotid-arteries). Smaller [groups](@head-nodes) under the jaw and chin, behind the ear and at the back of the head drain into it.

In a sore throat the node just below the angle of the jaw swells and aches, because the tonsil drains to it.

Low on the left, near the end of the [thoracic duct](@thoracic-duct), sits a node that can catch cancer cells carried up from the abdomen. When a cancer of the stomach enlarges it, doctors call it Virchow's node, after the pathologist who described it in 1848.
