Twenty to thirty [nodes](@axillary-nodes) lie in the fat of the armpit, around the [axillary vein](@axillary-veins) and [artery](@axillary-arteries) and under the [pectoralis major](@pectoralis-major). They drain the arm, the wall of the chest and the upper back, and they receive about three quarters of the lymph of the breast.

@image arm-lymphatics-gray-b488cd

A cancer of the breast spreads first to these nodes. To check them, a surgeon injects a dye or a tracer near the tumour and removes the first node it reaches, the sentinel node. If that node is clear, the others are very likely clear too and can stay.
