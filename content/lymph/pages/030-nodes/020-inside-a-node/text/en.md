A lymph node is a bean-shaped capsule, a few millimetres to two centimetres long. Several vessels bring lymph into its rounded side. Inside, the lymph seeps through channels lined with macrophages, which swallow bacteria, dust and debris. It leaves by one or two vessels at the hilum, the dent of the bean, where an artery and vein enter.

The outer layer, the cortex, holds round follicles of B cells. Deeper lies a zone of T cells, and in the middle, the medulla, plasma cells release antibodies into the outgoing lymph. Lymphocytes enter the node from the blood through the walls of its small veins, so the cells and the lymph meet here.
