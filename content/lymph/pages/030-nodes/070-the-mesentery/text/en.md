The [mesentery](@mesentery) is a fan-shaped fold that ties the small intestine to the back wall of the abdomen and carries its vessels. Between its two layers lie 100 to 150 [nodes](@mesenteric-nodes), strung along the branches of the [superior mesenteric artery](@superior-mesenteric-artery) and [vein](@superior-mesenteric-vein).

They take the lymph of the small intestine, white with fat after a meal, and whatever crossed the gut wall. From them it passes to [nodes in front of the aorta](@aortic-nodes), at the root of the artery, and on to the [cisterna chyli](@cisterna-chyli). In children an infection can swell these nodes and cause a pain easily taken for appendicitis.
