Beside the blood vessels runs a second network. It carries a clear fluid, lymph, one way only: from the tissues back to the blood. Its vessels begin as closed tubes in almost every tissue, pass through hundreds of filters, the [lymph nodes](@lymph-nodes), and join into the [thoracic duct](@thoracic-duct), which ends in the [great veins of the neck](@great-veins).

@image lymphatic-system-d37ec5

The same system holds the organs where white blood cells are made, trained and stored: the red marrow inside the [bones](@marrow-bones), the [thymus](@thymus) behind the breastbone and the [spleen](@spleen) under the left ribs. Its two jobs, draining fluid and fighting infection, share one set of vessels.

The nodes and ducts on the model are drawn. The nodes stand where an anatomist placed them in the Z-Anatomy atlas, and the ducts follow the course the anatomy texts give. Their shapes are simplified, and real nodes vary in size and number from one person to the next.
