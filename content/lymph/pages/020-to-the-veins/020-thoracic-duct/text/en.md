Lymph from the legs, the pelvis and the gut passes the [nodes beside the aorta](@aortic-nodes) and gathers in a sac a few centimetres long, the [cisterna chyli](@cisterna-chyli). It lies in front of the first two lumbar vertebrae, beside the [abdominal aorta](@abdominal-aorta). From its top rises the [thoracic duct](@thoracic-duct), the largest lymph vessel in the body, 38 to 45 cm long.

The duct passes through the diaphragm with the aorta and climbs the back of the chest between the [descending aorta](@descending-aorta) and the [azygos vein](@azygos-vein). Near the fifth thoracic vertebra it crosses to the left, rises into the root of the neck and arches over to end at the [left venous angle](@left-venous-angle), where the internal jugular and subclavian veins meet.

It carries the lymph of three quarters of the body, about 100 ml an hour at rest. A valve at its mouth keeps blood out.
