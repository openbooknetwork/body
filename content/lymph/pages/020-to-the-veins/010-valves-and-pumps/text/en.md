Larger lymph vessels have valves every few millimetres, so a filled vessel looks like a string of beads. Each segment between two valves has smooth muscle in its wall. When lymph stretches a segment, it contracts and pushes the lymph past the next valve, several times a minute.

Outside forces help. Muscles squeeze the vessels as they work, arteries pulse beside them, and the [diaphragm](@diaphragm) lowers the pressure in the chest with each breath, which draws lymph up the [thoracic duct](@thoracic-duct). At rest about 120 ml of lymph reaches the blood each hour. In hard exercise the flow can rise ten to thirty times.
