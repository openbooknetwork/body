In the wall of the [ileum](@ileum), the last part of the small intestine, lie oval patches of lymphoid tissue a centimetre or more long, on the side opposite the mesentery. Johann Conrad Peyer described them in 1677. Each patch is a cluster of follicles like those of a node, set just under the lining of the gut.

@image peyers-patch-gray-a243c2

Special cells in the lining over a patch take up samples of what passes in the gut and hand them to the lymphocytes beneath. The B cells there make IgA, an antibody that is secreted into the gut and binds germs before they can attach. The wall of the [appendix](@appendix) is packed with the same follicles.
