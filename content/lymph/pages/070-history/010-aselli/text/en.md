On 23 July 1622 in Milan, Gaspare Aselli, professor of anatomy at Pavia, opened a living dog that had just eaten, to show friends its nerves. In the [mesentery](@mesentery) he saw many fine white threads. He cut one and a milky fluid ran out. In a dog that had not eaten, the threads could not be seen.

@image aselli-lacteals-f48d00

He named them venae lacteae, milky veins. Following Galen, he thought they carried the chyle to the [liver](@liver) to be made into blood. Aselli died before he could publish, and his book De lactibus sive lacteis venis appeared in Milan in 1627, with the first anatomical plates printed in colour.
