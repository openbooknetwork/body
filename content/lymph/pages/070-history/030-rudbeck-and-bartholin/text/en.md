In Uppsala the young Swede Olof Rudbeck traced clear, watery vessels from the [liver](@liver) and from all over the body into the [thoracic duct](@thoracic-duct), and in 1652 he showed them to Queen Christina. In Copenhagen Thomas Bartholin found the same vessels and named them vasa lymphatica, water vessels, from the Latin lympha, clear water.

Both men published in 1653 and argued for years over who had been first. Bartholin added a mock funeral for the liver, which had lost the role Galen gave it as the maker of blood. Lymph and chyle, they had shown, reach the blood through the [great veins of the neck](@great-veins).
