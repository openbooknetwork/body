Jean Pecquet, a medical student at Montpellier, was dissecting a dog when he saw white chyle well up from the veins above its heart. He traced it down a [thin vessel](@thoracic-duct) along the spine to a [sac](@cisterna-chyli) on the lumbar vertebrae. He called it the receptacle of the chyle. Today it is the cisterna chyli.

@image cisterna-chyli-gray-592d5a

The vessel was the thoracic duct, and it ended in the [left subclavian vein](@left-subclavian-vein). The chyle went to the [superior vena cava](@superior-vena-cava) and the heart, and the liver had no part in it, against what Aselli and Galen had held. Pecquet published the finding in Paris in 1651, in his Experimenta nova anatomica.
