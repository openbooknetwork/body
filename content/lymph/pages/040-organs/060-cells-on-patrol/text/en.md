Only about 2 in 100 of the body's lymphocytes are in the blood at any moment. The rest sit in the [nodes](@lymph-nodes), the [spleen](@spleen), the marrow, the [thymus](@thymus) and the wall of the gut.

They keep moving. A lymphocyte leaves the blood inside a node, spends some hours there, and returns with the lymph through the [thoracic duct](@thoracic-duct) and the [great veins of the neck](@great-veins). Then it enters another node. In this way the few cells that recognize one germ can search the whole body for it.

When a lymphocyte meets its germ, it divides again and again. A node filling with dividing cells grows tender and swollen, the swollen gland of an infection.
