Every lymphocyte starts in the red marrow, which in an adult survives in [the skull, the breastbone, the vertebrae, the pelvis and the top of each thighbone](@marrow-bones). It comes from the same stem cells as red cells and platelets. The B cells finish their training in the marrow itself. Young T cells leave in the blood for the thymus.

B cells are named for the bursa of Fabricius, a pouch beside the gut of birds. In 1956 Bruce Glick found that chickens without one made no antibodies. Humans have no bursa, and the marrow does its work. Marrow and thymus make the lymphocytes. The nodes, spleen, tonsils and gut patches are where they meet germs.
