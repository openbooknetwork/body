Cut across, the [spleen](@spleen) is mostly red pulp, a sponge of blood-filled spaces where worn-out red cells are caught and broken down. Scattered through it are grey-white dots about a millimetre across, the white pulp. Each is a sheath of lymphocytes round a small artery, with T cells next to the artery and B cells in round follicles beside it.

The cut on the model crosses the upper abdomen at the level of the spleen, seen from above. Blood that enters the spleen passes the white pulp first, so a germ carried in the blood meets the lymphocytes there, and the B cells answer by making antibodies against it.
