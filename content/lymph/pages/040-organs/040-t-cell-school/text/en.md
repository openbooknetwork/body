Each lobe of the [thymus](@thymus) is split into lobules, each with a dark outer cortex packed with young T cells and a paler core, the medulla. Young cells arrive from the marrow. Each builds a receptor by joining gene pieces at random, so every cell recognizes a different shape, and most of those shapes are useless or dangerous.

@image thymus-openstax-6fdca7

The thymus tests them. A cell whose receptor cannot grip the body's own marker molecules dies in the cortex. A cell that grips the body's own proteins too hard dies in the medulla, since it would attack the body. More than nine in ten die there, and the rest enter the blood as mature T cells.

The cut on the model passes through both lobes. Under the microscope the medulla shows round whorls of flattened cells, Hassall's corpuscles, found only in the thymus.
