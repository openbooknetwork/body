The [spleen](@spleen) is the largest lymphoid organ, about 12 cm long and 150 g, tucked under the left ribs behind the [stomach](@stomach). It filters blood the way a node filters lymph. Blood arrives by the [splenic artery](@splenic-artery) and leaves by the [splenic vein](@splenic-vein) for the liver. Unlike a node, it has no lymph vessels bringing lymph in.

Its lymphocytes meet the germs that reach the bloodstream. A blow to the left ribs can tear the spleen, and it is then often removed. A person without a spleen lives well but is more open to some bacteria, such as the pneumococcus, and is vaccinated against them.
