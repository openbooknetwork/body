The [jejunum](@jejunum) and [ileum](@ileum) hang from the back wall of the abdomen on the [mesentery](@mesentery), a fan-shaped double sheet of peritoneum with fat between its layers. Its root on the back wall is only about 15 cm long, but its free edge carries the whole six metres of coiled gut, which lets the loops move as they work.

@image peritoneum-section-gray-f652ca

Between the two layers run the vessels, lymph nodes and nerves of the gut. The [superior mesenteric artery](@superior-mesenteric-artery) branches into arches that feed every loop. The blood that leaves, carrying the absorbed sugars and amino acids, gathers in the [superior mesenteric vein](@superior-mesenteric-vein) and flows on to the liver.

@image superior-mesenteric-artery-gray-249fac

In 2016 the surgeons Calvin Coffey and Peter O'Leary showed that the mesentery is one continuous structure from the duodenum to the rectum, and proposed that it be counted as an organ.
