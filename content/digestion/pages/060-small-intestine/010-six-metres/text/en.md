After the duodenum the small intestine coils in loops through the middle and lower abdomen, framed by the large intestine. Its first two fifths are the [jejunum](@jejunum), wider and with a thicker wall, and the last three fifths the [ileum](@ileum). Measured after death the whole is about six metres long, and in a living person less.

Here digestion is finished and nearly all the food is absorbed. Rings of muscle contract in segments that churn the chyme against the wall, and slow waves push it on at about a centimetre a minute. A meal takes three to five hours to travel from the pylorus to the large intestine.
