Glands outside the tube pour their juices into it through ducts. The [salivary glands](@salivary-glands) open into the mouth. The [liver](@liver) makes bile, which waits in the [gallbladder](@gallbladder) until a meal arrives. The [pancreas](@pancreas), behind the stomach, makes enzymes for every kind of food. Bile and pancreatic juice enter together at the start of the small intestine.

Millions of small glands in the wall of the stomach and intestine add their own. Together the glands pour about seven litres a day into the tube, far more than a person drinks. Nearly all of it is taken back: of the eight or nine litres that pass each day, about a tenth of a litre leaves in the faeces.
