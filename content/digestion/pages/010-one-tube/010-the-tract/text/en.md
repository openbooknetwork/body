Everything you eat passes through one tube from the mouth to the anus. Its parts follow each other in order: the [pharynx](@pharynx), the [esophagus](@esophagus), the [stomach](@stomach), the [small intestine](@small-intestine) and the [large intestine](@large-intestine). Measured after death, with its muscle slack, the small intestine alone is about six metres long and the large intestine one and a half.

@image digestive-tract-diagram-38ea2e

The tube does two kinds of work. Teeth and the muscle in its wall break up and mix the food. Enzymes from glands then cut it into molecules small enough to cross the wall into the blood: sugars, amino acids and fatty acids. What the body cannot use leaves at the far end, usually a day or two later.
