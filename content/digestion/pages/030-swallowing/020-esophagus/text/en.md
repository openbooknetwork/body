The [esophagus](@esophagus) is a muscular tube about 25 cm long. It runs down the neck behind the [trachea](@trachea), through the chest behind the [heart](@heart) and beside the [aorta](@aorta), and through the diaphragm into the stomach. Between swallows it lies flat and empty. Its upper third is skeletal muscle like that of the limbs, the rest smooth muscle like the gut's.

A swallow starts a ring of contraction that travels down the tube and drives the bolus ahead of it, a movement called peristalsis. The wave takes 8 to 10 seconds to reach the stomach, a little less when gravity helps. The muscle does the work, so a person can swallow upside down and astronauts can eat in orbit.
