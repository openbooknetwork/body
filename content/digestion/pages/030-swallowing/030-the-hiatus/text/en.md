The esophagus passes into the abdomen through an opening in the [diaphragm](@diaphragm), the hiatus, at the level of the tenth thoracic vertebra. Its last few centimetres form the lower esophageal sphincter, a ring of muscle that stays closed between swallows. It relaxes as a swallow arrives and lets the bolus into the [stomach](@stomach).

This closure, helped by the diaphragm round the hiatus, keeps acid in the stomach. The lining of the [esophagus](@esophagus) has little defence against acid. When stomach contents flow back they cause heartburn, a burning pain behind the breastbone. In a hiatus hernia part of the stomach slides up through the opening into the chest, and backflow becomes more likely.
