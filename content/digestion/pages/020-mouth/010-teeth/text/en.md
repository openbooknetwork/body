An adult has 32 teeth, eight in each half of each jaw: two [incisors](@incisors) at the front, one [canine](@canines), two [premolars](@premolars) and three [molars](@molars). The third molars, the wisdom teeth, often never come through, as in this model. A child has 20 milk teeth, which the permanent set begins to replace at about the age of six.

@image lower-teeth-gray-d588a2

The incisors cut, the canines grip and tear, and the premolars and molars crush and grind. The jaw muscles can close the teeth with a force of about 25 kg on the incisors and 90 kg on the molars. Chewing breaks food into small pieces with a large surface, which the enzymes can then reach.

@image teeth-jaws-gray-22afcf

Enamel, the white cap of each tooth, is about 96 percent mineral and the hardest tissue in the body. It holds no living cells, so once decay breaks through it the enamel cannot repair itself.
