The [pancreas](@pancreas) is a soft gland 12 to 15 cm long lying across the back of the abdomen behind the stomach. Its head sits in the curve of the [duodenum](@duodenum) and its tail against the [spleen](@spleen). Clusters of cells along its branching ducts make about a litre of juice a day, and the [pancreatic duct](@pancreatic-duct) carries it to the duodenum.

@image pancreas-duodenum-gray-40e703

The juice holds enzymes for every main food: trypsin and chymotrypsin for proteins, amylase for starch and lipase for fat. The protein enzymes leave the pancreas inactive and are switched on only in the duodenum. When they switch on inside the gland, it starts to digest itself, which is acute pancreatitis.

Scattered through the gland are 1 to 2 million islets of Langerhans. They make up one or two percent of its mass and send insulin and glucagon into the blood.
