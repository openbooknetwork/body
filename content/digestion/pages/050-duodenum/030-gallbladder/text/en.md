The liver makes 600 to 1,000 ml of bile a day, which drains down its [bile ducts](@liver-ducts). Between meals their outlet is closed, so bile backs up the [cystic duct](@cystic-duct) into the [gallbladder](@gallbladder), a pear-shaped bag under the [liver](@liver) that holds 30 to 60 ml. Its lining absorbs water and salt and concentrates the bile about five times.

@image gallbladder-ducts-gray-c7a78e

When fat reaches the duodenum, the hormone cholecystokinin makes the gallbladder contract and the outlet relax, and bile flows down the [bile duct](@bile-duct) into the [duodenum](@duodenum). Bile salts break fat into fine droplets that lipase can reach, then carry the products to the gut wall. Most bile salts are absorbed again in the ileum and used many times over.

When bile holds more cholesterol than its salts can keep dissolved, crystals form and grow into gallstones. Many people carry stones without knowing it. A stone that blocks a duct causes sharp pain under the right ribs.
