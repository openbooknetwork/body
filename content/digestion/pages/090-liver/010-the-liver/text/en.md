The [liver](@liver) is the largest gland in the body, about 1.5 kg in an adult. It fills the upper right of the abdomen under the dome of the [diaphragm](@diaphragm), sheltered by the lower ribs, and reaches across to the left above the stomach. The gallbladder hangs under its right lobe.

@image liver-upper-gray-c048bd

Surgeons divide the liver into eight segments, each with its own branch of the blood vessels and bile ducts, so that one can be removed and the others keep working. This model's liver is built from those eight. If up to 70 percent of a healthy liver is removed, the rest grows back to the former size.

Under the microscope the liver is made of 50,000 to 100,000 lobules, each one to two millimetres across. In each lobule, plates of liver cells run out from a central vein.
