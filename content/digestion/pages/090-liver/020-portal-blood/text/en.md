All the blood that leaves the stomach, intestines, pancreas and spleen passes through the liver before it reaches the heart. The [superior mesenteric vein](@superior-mesenteric-vein) and the [splenic vein](@splenic-vein) join behind the pancreas to form the [portal vein](@portal-vein), which carries about a litre a minute into the [liver](@liver). The hepatic artery adds some 300 ml a minute, rich in oxygen.

Inside the liver the blood seeps through wide capillaries past rows of liver cells. The cells store glucose as glycogen and release it between meals, make albumin and clotting proteins, turn ammonia into urea and break down alcohol and many drugs. The blood then gathers in the [hepatic veins](@hepatic-veins) and enters the [inferior vena cava](@inferior-vena-cava).

The liver also keeps stores: enough vitamin B12 for a year or more, enough vitamin A for about ten months, and iron.
