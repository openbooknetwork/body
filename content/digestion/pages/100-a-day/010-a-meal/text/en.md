A bite is chewed for a few seconds and swallowed in one, and the [digestive tract](@tract) takes over. It reaches the stomach about ten seconds later. The stomach churns it and over about four hours passes nearly all of it on. The small intestine takes another three to five hours and absorbs nearly everything the body can use.

The slowest part is the colon. In healthy adults its contents take on average about a day and a half to pass, and up to three days is normal. The remains of a meal leave the body a day or two after it was eaten, mixed with the remains of several others.
