Every 15 to 20 seconds a wave of contraction sweeps along the [stomach](@stomach) towards the pylorus. The antrum squeezes the food against the nearly closed outlet and throws most of it back, again and again. Mixed with acid and enzymes, the meal becomes chyme, a thick milky fluid.

The pylorus is a thick ring of muscle. It lets water out freely but holds back pieces larger than a millimetre or two, and each strong wave sends a few millilitres of chyme into the [duodenum](@duodenum). Within about four hours a healthy stomach has emptied more than nine tenths of a solid meal.

The duodenum sets the pace. Acid, fat and stretch in its wall slow the stomach through nerves and through hormones such as cholecystokinin, so a fatty meal stays longer.
