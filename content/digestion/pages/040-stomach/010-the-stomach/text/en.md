The [stomach](@stomach) is a J-shaped bag under the left ribs, between the [esophagus](@esophagus) and the [duodenum](@duodenum). Its dome, the fundus, rises above the entrance and usually holds a bubble of swallowed air. Below it lie the body and the antrum, which narrows to the pylorus, the outlet. Part of the stomach lies behind the [liver](@liver).

Empty, its lining lies in deep folds. As food arrives the wall relaxes, so the stomach takes in 0.8 to 1.5 litres before the pressure inside rises. The wall has three layers of smooth muscle, the innermost running obliquely, which lets the stomach knead its contents in every direction.
