The [large intestine](@large-intestine) holds most of the body's bacteria, about 38 trillion of several hundred species, roughly as many as the body's own cells. Together they weigh about 200 grams. Most of them live without oxygen.

@image e-coli-6508bd

They ferment the fibre that human enzymes cannot split and turn it into short fatty acids, which feed the cells of the colon lining. They also make vitamin K, some B vitamins and gas. Some 7 to 10 litres of gas enter the colon each day, most of it is absorbed, and about 0.6 litres is passed.

Faeces are about three quarters water. About a third of the solid part is dead bacteria, and the rest is fibre, fat, salts and shed cells. The brown colour comes from bile pigments that the bacteria change.
