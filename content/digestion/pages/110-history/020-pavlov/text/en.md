In St Petersburg in the 1890s Ivan Pavlov operated on dogs so that their digestive glands opened to the skin, and kept them healthy for years. In his best-known experiment the [esophagus](@esophagus) was divided in the neck and both ends brought out. Food the dog ate fell out before reaching the [stomach](@stomach), yet the stomach poured out juice within minutes.

@image pavlov-operating-cff926

When he cut the vagus nerves, the juice stopped. The sight, smell and taste of food start gastric secretion through the brain, a phase now known to give about 30 percent of the juice a meal produces. His laboratory sold the juice from these dogs as a remedy for indigestion.

In 1904 Pavlov received the Nobel Prize for his work on the physiology of digestion. His dogs also began to drip saliva from their [salivary glands](@salivary-glands) at signals that came before food, and he spent his later decades on these conditioned reflexes.
